Step 1 of 8

The cell

This is an example RoboScan steel cell inside a closed shop. A QIROX robot hangs from a CLOOS C-frame that rolls on a floor track. The beam lies on steel stands beside it.

Illustration, not to scale. The front wall is cut away to show the cell. RoboScan belongs indoors, dry and out of the weather. Your layout is drawn to your building before any quote.

Step 2 of 8

Set the beam

The crane sets the tacked beam on the stands, web flat. Tacks stay under 5 mm (0.20 in) and off the corners. The track has to be longer than the longest part.

Step 3 of 8

Scan it

The laser scanner rides on the C-frame. As the C-frame drives the length of the beam, it scans the web and every joint the robot is about to weld. The program is made from the scan, so nobody teaches points by hand.

Step 4 of 8

Check the joints

The scan finds the joints the laser can see. The operator checks them on the screen, then starts the weld. Joints on the underside are out of view, so the beam is turned for those.

Step 5 of 8

Weld

The robot welds each joint the scan found. The C-frame moves it along the beam from joint to joint.

Shown sped up. Weld time depends on your part.

Step 6 of 8

Check the welds

With the robot parked, the operator walks the beam and checks every weld by eye and with a gauge, then touches up anything that needs it. The scan finds the joints. It does not inspect finished welds.

This step is manual. Plan for it in the cycle.

Step 7 of 8

Option: positioners

Instead of stands, the beam can sit in two ring positioners. They turn the beam so the joints on the other side face the robot.

An option. Ask your JPS rep what fits your parts.
